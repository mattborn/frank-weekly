Takeaways:

- At pick 3, three of today’s top-4 RBs were still available: K. Walker (went 19), Bijan (5), CMC (6). JT is RB5. Fine, not optimal. Had the Colts a stronger offense he could be RB1-2.
- Adams was the best early pick. McConkey and BTJ the genuine busts.
- Corum at 99 was LAR’s depth-2 behind Kyren. Hubbard at 106 was CAR’s depth-1. Hubbard is RB14 (110.1), Corum RB22, and Corum only hit because the elite LAR backfield feeds two RBs. Rule: at equal cost, take the depth-1. This is also the perfect example of the team filter wrongly ruling out Carolina (2025 #24, now #3). The filter can’t see breakouts, so its cost concentrates in vetoing depth-1s on bad-last-year teams.
- New rule: the team filter is confirmed safe only on projected starters (Dak R6 hit). Every depth-chart application busted (Price R5, Gesicki). Stop applying it to backups in any round.

League-wide strategy patterns:
- Optimal shape is RB-heavy early, WR late. Top 3 PF teams all took 2 RBs in R1-4. I took 1, then hunted RBs in R9/R10/R13, where RB hit rate falls to 14% while WR holds 30%. Corum and Monangai beating those odds hides how bad that bet usually is.
- Dak R6 → QB6 was safe and efficient. Early QB was the riskiest move in the league: Allen R2 → QB1 paid, Lamar R2 → QB20 is the biggest positional bust. Waiting kept working: Lawrence R11 → QB9, and three teams fixed QB off the FA pool.
- Early WR buys ceiling, late WR buys floor. 10 of the top 12 WRs went in the first 4 rounds. The late market still hits 30% but produced only two difference-makers (Watson #72, Golden #139).
- TE waiting was viable this year: Likely R11 → TE2, Juwan Johnson went undrafted. Bowers R2 → TE4 hit, but the data says the premium was optional. My NYG-QB hesitation on Likely was reasonable. It resolved his way.
- Late rounds reward proven vets, not darts. Purdy R13 → QB2, Goff R14 → QB4, Love R14 → QB5, Stafford R10 → QB7, Aaron Jones R12 → RB19. Darts on players without an established role almost all went to zero (Lane, Concepcion, Stribling, Tyson, Boutte). Woody Marks RB24 is the lone exception. The Josh Jacobs play is the same bias: established role, cheap, hold until it resolves.
- The Bears bias. Four of us are Bears fans (Matten 27, gopadres, HeidisHouseOfPain, Indiana Bears) and it cuts twice. We hoard CHI: 7 Bears rostered across our 4 teams vs 3 across the other 8, and we got the wrong ones (Loveland TE13, Caleb QB17) while non-fans took the hits (Swift RB9, Burden WR16). We fade GB: all three GB difference-makers went to non-fans (Watson WR3 #72, Golden WR9 #139, Love QB5 R14). My burn history (Golden, Doubs, Reed) was real but it flipped. All four fans are under .500. Same lesson as McCaffrey.
- Nobody benefited from early K/DEF. The best cases (SEA DEF R9 → DEF1, Myers R9 → K2) were still dominated by waiting: Reichard K1 went #174, Santos K3 #176, Vikings DEF4 R14. The positional edge is ~10-20 pts over 7 weeks while a R6-9 skill pick is a coin flip at 60-130. My Aubrey R8 → K4 lost to Reichard 86 picks later.
